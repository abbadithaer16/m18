Leon Vasari

Director, 1958 to 1989. Twelve features, most of them about people waiting for something that does not arrive: a boat, a letter, a reason.

Leon Vasari is a fictional director. This biography, his films and their dates are invented for a design study.

1950s

Beginnings

Vasari grew up between a port town and a mountain village, and spent his twenties writing subtitles for other people’s films. He learned editing by cutting their translations to fit the picture.

His first feature, The Silent Valley, was shot in thirty-one days with a borrowed camera and a cast of schoolteachers. It set the pattern for the next three decades: small places, long silences, and a secret everyone already knows.

1960s

Water and fire

Through the sixties Vasari followed water. A harbour at dusk in The Blue Hour, a salt barge on a dying river, a village waiting for a dam to drown it.

He began working with the same small crew he would keep for the rest of his career, and with a cinematographer who refused to light anything that the sun could not.

1970s

The long middle

His most productive decade. Noon in Ashes turned a western into a ghost story; The Observer made a thriller out of a building that never moves.

Light in the Storm, at two hours seven minutes, is his longest film and the one he said he would keep if he could keep only one.

1980s

Last reels

In the eighties his films grew quieter and stranger. A Quiet War is the only comedy he made, about a feud nobody can explain.

He ended with Red Door, a film about the last night of a cinema, and then stopped. He never announced a retirement; he simply did not start another film.
